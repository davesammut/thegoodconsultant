Fire-seeking: the hidden skill of technical leadership

Draft article · 5 min read

Good technical leaders do not just put out fires. They develop the judgement, curiosity, and courage to find them before they become crises.

Comfort is not the same as control

Healthy delivery environments can still conceal risk. A stable roadmap, familiar architecture, and experienced team may reduce visible disruption while allowing assumptions, duplicated logic, weak ownership, or organisational friction to accumulate.

Fire-seeking is the deliberate practice of looking beyond the status report. It means following weak signals, listening for contradictions, and asking what everyone else has learned to work around.

A practical posture

  • Observe: Look for recurring friction, silence, unexplained workarounds, and decisions that are repeatedly deferred.
  • Challenge: Test the dominant story without turning the conversation into a contest.
  • Change: Convert concern into a hypothesis, an owner, and the next safe action.

The point is not heroics

Fire-seeking is not about becoming indispensable. It is about making the invisible visible, sharing ownership, and strengthening the system so fewer problems depend on individual intervention.
