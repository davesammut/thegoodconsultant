Notes from the field

Every organisation tells itself a story

5 min read

Ask a leadership team what kind of organisation they are, and you'll get an answer fast. "We move quickly." "We're data-driven." "We're an engineering-led culture." The confidence of the answer rarely matches the evidence behind it.

That's not dishonesty. It's something more interesting.

The story becomes the evidence

Once an organisation settles on a story about itself, that story starts doing work. It shapes which facts get repeated in meetings and which get quietly dropped. A missed deadline becomes "a one-off," because the story says the team moves quickly. A gut decision gets described as "informed by the data," because the story says the culture is data-driven.

None of this requires anyone to lie. It only requires everyone to prefer the version that fits.

Why it's worth noticing

The story an organisation tells about itself is one of the fastest things to learn about it, and one of the least examined. It's usually more stable than the roadmap, more defended than the strategy, and more revealing than the mission statement.

You can hear it within the first hour of any conversation with a leadership team, if you listen for the phrase people reach for without thinking.

The question that actually helps

If you want to understand an organisation quickly, don't ask what it's proud of. Ask what it's stopped mentioning. The gap between the two is usually where the real work is.
